Six weeks later it launched in Canada, the UK and Ireland at once.

**Caption**
The brief for the 10th Selangor Super Junior Circuit in Malaysia was simple: make it feel like a game.

These kids play squash at national level, and they're also the generation that grew up on loading screens and XP bars. So we built a whole gaming world for them. Two legs, two colour systems, one visual language that ran from the entrance banner to the bunting, jerseys, lanyards and 48 medals.

We also assigned the colours on purpose so they didn't follow the usual "blue for boys, pink for girls" habit of junior sport.

Parents photographed the entrance banner. The players wore their shirts home. That's the part we're proudest of.

## Post 3: Nandhini Design Constructions carousel (tip post)

**Hook options**
- A. Three people visit your website. Each wants a different answer.
- B. Your website has three visitors. Are you talking to all of them?
- C. Before we designed an interior studio's site, we asked who actually visits it.

My pick is A.

**Slides**
1. Headline from the hook. Image: Nandhini home page on laptop.
2. "Visitor 1: a family planning a home. Their question: can this studio design a space that feels like us?"
3. "Visitor 2: a friend of a past client. Their question: what have they actually done?"
4. "Visitor 3: a contractor or supplier. Their question: are they credible to work with?"
5. "So we built the site around trust, in the order those questions get asked." Image: project pages and contact section screenshots.
6. "Try this on your own site: write down your three visitors and the one question each one has. If the answer isn't on your home page, that's the fix. nandhinidc.in, designed and built by NicheUX."

**Caption**
When we designed the website for Nandhini Design Constructions, an interior design studio in India, we didn't start with colours or layouts. We started with people.

Three kinds of visitor land on an interior studio's site, and each arrives with one question. A family wants to know if the studio can create a home that feels like theirs. A friend of a past client wants to see the work. A contractor wants to know if the studio is serious.

Every section of the site answers one of those questions, in the order people ask them.

Try it with your own business this week. Who are your three visitors, and does your home page answer each of them?

See the live site: nandhinidc.in
